# Chapter 4: Thermonuclear Reactions II 
This class discusses some fundamental concepts of thermonuclear reaction rates and their role in nucleosynthesis. We start by defining nuclear reaction rates and deriving the reaction-rate expressions for charged-particle reactions using the **Maxwell–Boltzmann velocity distribution**. Next we explore neutron-induced reactions, highlighting the absence of the Coulomb barrier and the importance of neutron capture in the synthesis of heavy elements through the s-process and r-process. The Maxwellian Averaged Cross Section (MACS) is derived and its significance in astrophysical reaction networks is discussed. Then, gamma-induced reactions are explored. The photon distribution in stellar plasmas is described using **Planck’s radiation law**, leading to the derivation of the photodisintegration reaction rate and the gamma-induced decay constant. The connection between radiative capture reactions and their inverse photodisintegration processes is emphasized, together with their importance in high-temperature stellar environments and explosive nucleosynthesis.

The final part of the chapter focuses on **inverse reactions** and the principle of detailed balance. The concepts of forward and inverse reactions, Q-values, compound nuclei, time-reversal invariance, and the reciprocity theorem are introduced. Relationships between forward and inverse cross-sections and reaction rates are derived, demonstrating how reaction equilibria emerge in stellar plasmas. Particular attention is given to the strong temperature dependence of inverse reactions and their role in environments such as novae, X-ray bursts, supernovae, and nuclear statistical equilibrium.

By the end of this chapter you should understand how reaction rates are calculated for charged-particle, neutron-induced, and gamma-induced reactions, how forward and inverse reactions are connected through detailed balance and why these concepts are essential for understanding stellar evolution, energy production, and the origin of the elements in the Universe.
